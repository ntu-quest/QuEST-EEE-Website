QuEST@EEE is the quantum club at NTU's School of Electrical & Electronic Engineering: workshops, live demos and research projects, with no background needed to start.

Try a demo Start from nothing

Don't follow the path, explore it. QuEST@EEE began as NTU's Quantum Computing Club. Now we teach quantum from zero, build our own demos, and take students to research-grade work.

Who we are. Students of NTU's School of EEE and anyone else curious enough to come, run by a student committee and advised by faculty. Meet the people.

What we do. Workshops that assume nothing, live physics demos, and mentored projects in quantum algorithms, software and hardware. What that looks like.

Where. On NTU's campus in the South Spine, and further out: Quantum Fall Fest ran across NTU, NUS and the Centre for Quantum Technologies.

How to reach us. Sessions are announced in the Telegram community; we post on Instagram; and you can email the President.

Why quantum, and why now

In 1900 Planck found that energy comes in lumps. By 1927 quantum mechanics had been written down, and its founders were already arguing about what it meant. In 1964 Bell turned the argument into a measurement, and experiments have sided with quantum mechanics ever since.

In the 1980s Feynman asked what a computer built from quantum parts could do; in the 1990s Shor and Grover answered with algorithms. This year, an error-corrected trapped-ion machine computed the energy of a hydrogen molecule.

The whole century, year by year

  • It is engineering now

    Microwave and optical control chains, cryogenics, and the measurement electronics between a qubit and a number on a screen. That is electrical engineering, which is why the club sits in EEE. The Quantum Stack

  • The uses are real, the doubts too

    Molecular energies have the clearest case, and cryptography is already changing. Where nobody yet knows whether a quantum method beats a classical one, that is the answer we give. What we believe

  • Anyone can start

    Every session assumes you have not seen it before. The people teaching were in the audience two semesters ago, which is the strongest argument we have that this is learnable. Start from nothing

From Planck to us

A century of quantum mechanics, then quantum information and computing. Keep scrolling and the line runs into our own story.

1900

  1. Quantum mechanics

    1900–1939

  3. 1900

    Max Planck: energy comes in lumps

    To explain how hot objects glow, Planck assumes energy is given off in discrete packets: quanta.

  5. 1922

    Stern and Gerlach split a beam of silver atoms in two

    Sent through a magnet, the atoms land in two spots and never in between. Spin is quantised.

    Station three re-runs it

  6. 1925–26

    Heisenberg, Schrödinger and Born write it down

    Matrix mechanics, the wave equation, and Born's rule: the square of an amplitude is a probability.

    Station one draws amplitudes

  7. 1927

    The fifth Solvay Conference argues over what it means

    Heisenberg's uncertainty principle is months old. The argument about measurement is still running.

    We held one in 2026

  9. 1935

    Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen ask if the theory is complete

    Particles that have interacted stay correlated however far apart they go. Schrödinger names it entanglement that year.

  10. 1939

    Paul Dirac introduces bra–ket notation

    ⟨φ|ψ⟩ for an amplitude. The brackets around our name are his.

  11. Testing the strangeness

    1964–1982

  13. 1964

    John Bell shows the difference can be measured

    No local hidden-variable theory can reproduce every prediction of quantum mechanics, and an inequality tells them apart.

    Station two

  14. 1969

    Clauser, Horne, Shimony and Holt make it an experiment

    Score the correlations: anything classical tops out at 2.

    Station two keeps that score

  16. 1972–82

    Freedman and Clauser, then Aspect, run the test

    Nature goes past 2. Aspect's 1982 experiments switch the detectors while the photons are in flight.

  17. Information and computing

    1982–2019

  19. 1982

    Richard Feynman: build the computer out of quantum parts

    Classical machines struggle to simulate quantum systems, so simulate them with a quantum one.

    Our first workshop starts here

  20. 1984

    Bennett and Brassard publish BB84

    A key shared with single photons, where an eavesdropper leaves a trace: the first quantum cryptography protocol.

  22. 1994

    Peter Shor factors numbers, on paper

    A fast quantum algorithm for factoring, the problem behind most public-key cryptography.

  23. 1995

    A word, a code and a gate

    Schumacher names the qubit, Shor shows quantum information can be protected, and Monroe and Wineland run a two-qubit gate on trapped ions.

  24. 1996

    Lov Grover finds a faster way to search

    One marked item among N, found in about the square root of N steps. Boyer, Brassard, Høyer and Tapp give the exact count in 1998.

    Station one Grover's paper Boyer et al.'s paper

  26. 2007

    Singapore's Centre for Quantum Technologies opens

    A national research centre at NUS. The Fall Fest cohort visited it in 2025.

    Quantum Fall Fest

  28. 2014

    Peruzzo and colleagues: the variational eigensolver

    A small quantum processor and a classical optimiser share the work of finding a molecule's energy, first on a photonic chip.

    Station fourPeruzzo et al.'s paper

  29. 2016

    O'Malley and colleagues compute H₂ on superconducting qubits

    Their measured Hamiltonian is the table station four runs on.

    Station fourO'Malley et al.'s paper

  30. 2019

    Google's Sycamore claims a beyond-classical result

    53 superconducting qubits and one sampling task. Whether it counts was the question at the Fall Fest panel.

    The panel

  31. Now

    2022–2026

  33. 2022

    Nobel Prize to Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger

    For experiments with entangled photons that established the violation of Bell inequalities.

    Station two

  34. 2025

    The International Year of Quantum

    A century after Heisenberg's 1925 paper.

  35. 2026

    Error-corrected chemistry on trapped ions

    Yamamoto and colleagues compute the energy of H₂ on Quantinuum's H2-2, with error-corrected qubits.

    Our fifth demo re-runs their circuit

1900UsSwipe through time

QuEST@EEE began in 2024–25 as the NTU Quantum Computing Club, QCC@NTU: Singapore's first undergraduate, student-run quantum-centric club, with a foundational workshop series and one aim, to lower the barrier to entry. In December 2025 it ran Quantum Fall Fest Singapore, five days across NTU and NUS as part of the global Qiskit Fall Fest, with a visit to the Centre for Quantum Technologies. The whole story

Through 2025–26 it ran a year-long quantum machine learning programme with MLDA@EEE: two teams of six, taken from no quantum background to a research-grade deliverable. In 2026 the club became Quantum Engineering, Science & Technology @ EEE, a remit wider than computing alone that spans software, physics and hardware, and opened the Quantum Questions series with Quantum × Philosophy.
