02 — Principles

Eight principles, and what each one costs you

Apple's Design principles give the spine. They are written for apps, so each is restated below as something checkable on a student club's website — then the four commercial jobs from the Bike Bear reference sit underneath, because a club site still has to recruit.

01 · Purpose
Make something meaningful

Every page names its job before it is built. "What is this page for?" has one answer, not four. The test: if a page cannot state its purpose in a sentence, it does not ship.

02 · Agency
Let people do things their own way

No forced funnel. Someone who already codes must be able to reach a project in one click without reading the beginner copy. Every interactive thing — the demos above all — is reversible: reset, undo, change the knob back.

03 · Responsibility
Act in people's best interest

No tracking beyond a privacy-preserving page count. No cookie banner because there are no cookies. Faces and emails published only with consent, with a stated removal path. Member data stays off a public site.

04 · Familiarity
Build on what people know

Conventional nav, conventional links, conventional forms. Spend novelty on the background and the demos; spend none of it on where the menu is.

05 · Flexibility
Adapt to diverse contexts

Phone first, because Instagram is the front door. Keyboard, screen reader and reduced-motion are designed in, not retrofitted. Every canvas has a text equivalent.

06 · Simplicity
Be clear and direct

Simplicity isn't minimalism. Keep the useful things close and let the rest fall away — which is why the homepage is eight sections and not fifteen, and why hierarchy is enforced by type scale rather than by boxes.

07 · Craft
Care about every detail

The mark does not shift size when the theme changes. Photographs share a crop. Numbers are tabular. This is the principle most visible to the professor you are trying to impress.

08 · Delight
Make it human — but not decorative

Name the feeling: invitation, not awe. The field in the header responds to you; the demos answer you. Apple's own warning is the rule — don't mistake delight for decoration. If an effect does not carry meaning, it goes.

The four jobs, underneath

Every page takes at least one, and none takes all four.

JobWhat it means here
InformAnswer the question the visitor arrived with, above the fold, before any manifesto.
CaptureGet an email or a Telegram join. The site's real asset is a list you can reach in October. Low-commitment capture beats "Join QuEST" for a first-time visitor.
ConvertMove someone one rung up the ladder in §05. Each page names the next rung, not the last one.
ExpressProve the club is real: photographs of actual sessions, named projects, dated events. Proof beats adjectives.

Eight rules that follow

  1. The 3 / 30 / 3 test. In 3 seconds a visitor knows what QuEST is; in 30, what they would do here; in 3 minutes they have found their specific thing and acted.
  2. One primary action per screen. Two equal buttons is zero primary buttons.
  3. Navigation is the journey, not the org chart. Labels match what a visitor wants, not which department owns the page.
  4. Tokens before pages. The design system in §10 is built first; a club site accumulates five authors across two committees.
  5. Every campaign gets a landing page with one goal and no nav — recruitment, Fall Fest, each workshop — reachable by a QR code on a poster.
  6. Mobile is the target, not the fallback. Both diagrams from the draft become responsive SVG with a list fallback; a fixed-width <pre> block is a broken page on a phone.
  7. Proof within one scroll of every claim. Unsupported claims get cut, not softened.
  8. Boring where it counts. Bold in voice, photography and the header field; conventional in nav, forms and links.

The draft contains the right idea twice — the CURIOUS → CONTRIBUTE ladder, and the membership lifecycle in the Structure Framework. They are the same ladder seen from outside and inside. Every page names the rung it serves and links to the next.

Rung 5

Literature → implementation → analysis → output.

/research
Rung 6

Design consequence

This is why the site needs an entry-point router rather than a single funnel — Apple's Agency principle in practice. Someone entering at rung 4 should never be made to read rung 1 copy. That is what /explore/resources is for, and why it is worth more effort than it looks.

EUSO process

Publicity reaches the staff advisor at least 3 working days before dissemination with the poster and a sign-off, and events need ≥10 participants excluding the committee. Build the event page and its short link into that submission, not after it.

/explore/resources Resources router Inform · Express

The page that makes the site useful before anyone joins — the highest return per hour of effort on the site.

  1. Five entry points — "I know nothing about quantum" · "I can code" · "I want to do research" · "I want to build hardware" · "I want the theory". Links to anchored tracks, not modals.
  2. Per track — 5–8 items in order with an honest time estimate, mixing QuEST's own slides and notebooks with external courses. Own material first, clearly badged.

  2. Department groups — one line on what the department does, then its people.
  3. Closing line — "We're students too. We're learning, experimenting, failing, and figuring things out alongside everyone else."

The constraint that shapes everything

The booth build must keep working from a USB stick with no wifi — that is what it is for. One source of truth, two outputs: physics.js and mathfmt.js stay shared and unmodified; only the page shell differs. Never fork the physics.

Integration: port the shell, share the logic

OptionCostWhat it gives up
A · iframe the booth filesAn afternoonThe outline rail, deep links, search indexing, mobile layout, theme sync, analytics. A stopgap, not the answer.
B · port the shell, share the logic (recommended)~1 week for Grover, ~2 days each afterNothing that matters. Controls and canvases are plain DOM, so the markup pastes in near-verbatim; the shell is written once for all four.
C · rewrite as componentsWeeksTime you do not have, and the offline build, which would drift immediately.

The flat page, and the problem with flattening it

The booth pages hide derivations behind a "Show the maths" disclosure and fit knobs and plots in one screen. The web version flattens that — everything expanded, in document order, with a sticky outline rail. Right for reading and for search, and it breaks the demo: once the page is four screens long the reader scrolls the knobs away and the plot stops answering.

Design fix — the one thing to get right

Pin the controls, not just the outline. On desktop the knob panel docks to the bottom of the viewport and stays there the whole way down, so a reader mid-derivation can still move the step dial and watch the curve answer. On mobile it becomes a collapsed bar with a drag handle. This is the difference between a demo and a textbook with a picture in it.

Canonical section order — one outline shape for all four

1 The hook Eyebrow, headline, one-sentence promise, [Run one step] [Reset] 2 Readout The big number + status pill 3 What you are seeing Primary canvas 4 The same thing, differently Second canvas — the geometric view 5 The whole picture Third canvas — success against steps, angle sweep, convergence 6 Controls Anchor target; the dock itself is sticky 7 The maths Flattened from <details> 8 The circuit The existing SVG with its hotspots 9 References Grover 1996 · Nielsen & Chuang · Bell 1964 · CHSH 1969 · ... 10 Take it further New — the activity_sheet notebook, the repo, the offline bundle

The outline rail is generated from the article's own headings with scroll-spy, so it cannot drift from the page. Below 900px it collapses to a sticky chip strip — never a hamburger inside a hamburger.

Four upgrades the web version should earn

  1. Deep-linkable state. /demos/grover?n=4&steps=9&marked=6 loads the overshoot case directly. A lecturer can set a problem by URL — this is what turns the demos into teaching infrastructure.
  2. Booth mode. ?kiosk=1 hides the nav, enlarges type, resets after two minutes idle. One codebase for the site and the physical booth.
  3. The offline bundle stays downloadable. A zip of booth/ on /demos. This is what lets an SQYC school run your stations at their own event.
  4. Link each demo to its notebook and its stack node. The rung-3 → rung-4 handoff, and what makes the Quantum Stack diagram concrete.
Blocks publication

QC_club_Demo has no licence file. Add one — MIT for the code, CC BY 4.0 for the explanatory text — or nobody can legally reuse the demos, including the schools you want running them.

Photos: the placeholder that is not a broken image

Until the shoot happens, every person gets a monogram tile — initials in Manrope, centred in exactly the box a photograph will occupy. Same aspect ratio, same grid position, so swapping in a photo is a content change, not a layout change.

  • Tint, not colour-coding. Four faint washes drawn from the brand hues, assigned deterministically from the name, so the grid varies without implying a category. Never a grey silhouette, never an emoji, never an AI portrait.
  • One code path. person.photo empty renders the monogram automatically — nobody has to remember to delete a placeholder.
  • Accessibility. The tile is aria-hidden; the name sits in real text beside it. A photograph gets alt text of the form "Portrait of Sahana Vamsee, Honorary General Secretary".
  • When you shoot: one session, one background, one crop for all ten. Consistent portraits read as a team; cropped Instagram photos read as a group chat. Removing the pressure to ship mismatched photos early is the whole point of the placeholder.
  • One line each on what they are working on this year. The difference between a directory and an introduction.

Emails: publish, but deliberately

Nine ExCo addresses are a deliberate exception to the no-personal-emails rule — an officer's address is part of holding the office. It does not extend to sub-committee members.

  1. Obfuscate the mailto:. Assemble the address at runtime from data attributes. Costs nothing; keeps the committee out of the scraped lists that feed student-targeted phishing.
  2. Prefill the subject. ?subject=QuEST%20—%20joining%20a%20project measurably improves what lands in the inbox.
  3. Ask about role aliases. quest-academics@ survives the annual handover; a personal address does not.

09 — Migration

What survives from the old site, and what does not

The old site is one dark page in Inter with four anchors. Its markup is not worth keeping. Its photographs, its three initiatives and its three proof figures are — and they are the fastest content wins available.

The photo library, and what each one is for

Ten files in assets/. This is a better set than most student clubs have, and none of it is currently doing any work.

The remaining four: fallfest_1.JPG (a speaker at a lectern — events gallery), mile_gu.jpg (a guest-speaker portrait — permission needed, or link to his faculty page instead), mlda_qcc_1.png (the VQE loop diagram — illustration on the VQE demo and its project page), and workshop_image_1.jpeg (a Bloch-sphere teaching slide — see the consent flag below).

Before any of these are published

Weight. Two files are 3936×2624 at 5–7 MB. Shipped as-is they would be the slowest thing on the site by an order of magnitude. Resize to 2000px wide max, export AVIF with a WebP fallback, and serve a 640px variant to phones. Target under 180 KB each.

Faces. fallfest_2 shows ~25 identifiable people and workshop_image_1 includes a video-call grid with names attached to faces. Get consent for the group photo, and crop the participant thumbnails out of the slide image entirely. fallfest_3 shows a slide containing third-party portraits — check before republishing.

Attribution. Record who took each photograph in the image's frontmatter now, while anyone still remembers.

The GIF library nobody is using

10 — Identity

The mark, the palette and the type

The mark is ⟨Qu|EST@EEE⟩ — a bra-ket enclosing a Q with a dagger. Dirac notation and a Hermitian conjugate, drawn as a monogram. It is a better piece of design than the site it is missing from, and the whole visual identity should be derived from it rather than invented alongside it.

Two files, two modes, one size

quest-logo-light.pnguse on LIGHT grounds
deep violet · slate · ice

quest-logo-dark.pnguse on DARK grounds
ice · periwinkle · indigo

The trap in the filenames

The files are named by mode, not by ink. quest-logo-light.png is the light-mode artwork and is drawn dark; quest-logo-dark.png is the dark-mode artwork and is drawn pale. Swap them and both marks lose their contrast — the dark-mode mark's pale "EST" washes out on paper, and the light-mode mark's deep-violet "Qu" disappears into ink. Put that sentence in a comment above the <picture> element.

One thing visible in the pair above even when they are used correctly: "EST" is the weakest element in the light-mode mark — pale, outlined, and the first part to disappear as the mark shrinks or is printed. Two consequences: never render the gradient mark below about 96px, and make the flat one-colour version below a priority rather than a nicety.

make_logos.py already crops both to their real artwork and pads them onto one shared 1114×624 canvas, so a fixed height renders an identical mark either way. That is a piece of craft worth protecting: do not re-export either file independently. If the mark changes, regenerate both through that script.

Usage rules

  • Large. Hero lockup 140–200px tall on desktop, 96–120px on mobile. Nav 40–48px. The mark carries the brand; a 24px version in a corner does not.
  • Theme swap via <picture> with prefers-color-scheme plus a [data-theme] override, so an explicit toggle beats the OS setting in both directions.
  • Clear space of at least the bracket's height on every side. Never on a photograph, never on a gradient, never rotated or recoloured.
  • Commission a flat single-colour version for the favicon, the OG image, small nav sizes and anything printed one-colour. The gradient mark does not survive being 32px or being photocopied. This is a small job for the Publicity Lead and it removes a whole class of future problems.
  • The wordmark in running text is "QuEST@EEE" in Manrope — never the image, never letterspaced differently per page.

Reconciling the gradient

The mark is a metallic gradient; the earlier draft of this document banned gradients. Both can be true: the mark keeps its gradient, the interface stays flat.   • One scale, no improvisation: 78 / 46 / 32 / 25 / 20 / 18 / 16 / 14 / 12.5 / 11. If a size is not on the scale it does not get used.

  • Two web-font families plus one mono, variable where available, display=swap, preconnected, subset to Latin. Everything else is a real fallback stack — the fallback is a system serif or sans, never a silent substitution.
11 — The field

The interactive background

The header of this page is the proposal, running. Two coherent sources emit into a plane; where the crests meet, the field is bright; where a crest meets a trough, it cancels. Move the pointer and you move the second source. Drag the λ slider and the fringes change spacing.

Why interference and not particles

The old site's hero is a decorative circuit with a glow filter. A drifting particle field would be the same mistake in a newer costume: atmosphere with no referent, on the banned list, and indistinguishable from every other technology site. Interference is different in three ways that matter.

It means something
It is the physics, not a mood

Superposition and interference are the actual subject. The bright and dark fringes are the same phenomenon the Bell and Grover demos are about — the background is a quiet first lesson, not a screensaver.

It answers
The visitor is one of the two sources

Apple's Agency principle at its smallest scale: the page responds to you within the first second, before you have clicked anything. It costs the visitor nothing and it is remembered.

It is quiet
Low contrast, low motion, low cost

Rendered at a fraction of the display resolution into a small buffer and scaled up, so it is cheap on a phone. Never above 10% contrast against the ground; text always wins.

It is honest
Delight is not decoration

Apple's own warning. The field earns its place because it is the club's subject and it is interactive. If either were untrue it would be cut — and it is cut, automatically, whenever it would cost the reader anything.

Specification

AspectRule
WhereHomepage hero and the /demos index header only. Never behind running text, never on /about, /join, an event page or a form.
RenderA canvas at ~1/6 display resolution (≈200×120 buffer) scaled up; the resulting softness is the aesthetic. One ImageData write per frame, no per-pixel canvas calls.
ColourThe indigo and ice tokens at ≤10% alpha over the page ground, read from the CSS custom properties so it follows the theme automatically. No third colour, no gradient stops, no glow.
MotionCapped at 30 fps. Phase advances slowly enough that the pattern reads as still at a glance. Paused by IntersectionObserver when scrolled out of view, and on document.hidden.
InteractionPointer position drives the second source, eased with a lag so it feels like a field rather than a cursor trail. Touch: the source follows a tap. Keyboard: no interaction required — the field is decorative in the strict accessibility sense and is aria-hidden.
Reduced motionprefers-reduced-motion: reduce → draw one frame and stop. No animation loop is ever started. The still frame is the design, not a degraded version of it.
Low powerSkip the loop entirely below 720px, on navigator.hardwareConcurrency ≤ 4, or when navigator.connection.saveData is set. A static frame in all three cases.
At restThe first frame is drawn synchronously on load, before any animation. A screenshot, a link preview and a reader who never moves the pointer all see the finished design.
BudgetUnder 2 KB of JavaScript. If it grows past that, it is doing too much.
Two variants worth prototyping

Sources on the page's own content. Anchor the two sources to the two hero buttons, so the fringes shift as the layout responds — the field becomes a property of the page rather than an overlay on it.

A real Grover run. Drive the field from the club's own physics.js: one Grover iteration every few seconds, amplitudes as the field. Ties the background to the demos and costs no new code. Riskier — it can read as a loading animation, so prototype before committing.

The line not to cross

One interactive background, in one place, at one intensity. The moment a second animated effect appears elsewhere on the site, both become decoration and the argument in this section stops being true.

13 — Content model

Content model

Content lives in Markdown with typed frontmatter, not in code. This is what lets a non-developer add an event without touching a component — the single most important decision for the site's survival past this committee.

project

title, slug, summary (≤160 chars), area [algorithms|software|research|hardware|photonics|security], difficulty [intro|intermediate|advanced], status [open|active|completed|paused], team [names], mentors [names], started, repo, outputs [{label,url}], cover, featured

event

title, slug, series [workshop|guest-lecture|showcase|industry-talk|hackathon|social], start, end, venue, format, difficulty, audience, learningOutcomes [], registerUrl, capacity, photos [], recap, status

person

name, preferredName ("Mon", "Thank", "Jerry"), role, roleShort, department [academics|projects-research|business-outreach|publicity|operations|exco], team, year, photo (empty renders the monogram), email, blurb (10–15 words), links [github|linkedin], active

demo

title, slug, station (1–4), concept, hook, stackNodes [], knobs [{label,range}], sections [] (what the outline rail renders), references [{citation,url}], offlineBundle, workshop (linked notebook)

link

label, description, url, icon [telegram|form|instagram|linkedin|email|github|calendar], order, surfaces [links|join|footer], live, expiresOn (a cycle or workshop disappears on its own)

media

file, alt (required — the build fails without it), credit, takenOn, consent [granted|pending|na], people [names, for takedown requests], usage [hero|gallery|illustration|history]

resource · milestone

resource: title, track, order, type, source [quest|external], url, estimatedTime, why milestone: year, title, blurb, photo, links []
Two rules

Every type carries active or status, and the site filters on it — nothing is deleted at handover, so the site becomes the club's institutional memory. And media.alt is required at the schema level: a missing alt text fails the build rather than shipping.
